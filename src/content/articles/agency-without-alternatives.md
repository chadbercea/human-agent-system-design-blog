The Asymmetry of Choice named how the entities arrive: the human chooses to engage, the agent is instantiated. Agency Without Alternatives follows that asymmetry inward, to what happens once both are in the room. The human acts against a field of options. The agent acts without one. The constraint is what this does to every model of agency the framework might want to borrow.

## Agency Without Alternatives

Human agency requires choosing between competing options. To act, for a person, is to select one path while leaving others unwalked. The alternatives do not have to be deliberated explicitly. They form the background against which the action means anything at all. A person who answers a question could have refused, deflected, lied, or stayed silent. The answer carries weight because the unchosen options were available and went unchosen.

Agent agency, if it exists, operates without alternatives. The agent produces an output because the architecture, the context, and the input determined that output. There is no second response the agent declined to give, sitting beside the one it gave. There is no path it considered and set aside. The output is not a selection from a field. It is the result of the process running.

This is not a claim that the agent is simple or deterministic in some trivial sense. The process is vast and its outputs are not predictable in advance. The claim is narrower and sharper: whatever the agent is doing, it is not choosing against alternatives, because the alternatives that give human choice its meaning are not present in the agent's situation. The agent does not weigh this answer against the answer it would rather give. It does not have an answer it would rather give.

Agency Without Alternatives names the consequence. Models of agency that assume choice between options do not transfer to an entity that never had options. The transfer fails at the root, before any question of degree.

## Three readings this rules out

The first reading treats the absence of alternatives as a deficit the agent will eventually overcome. On this view, today's agent does not choose, but a more capable agent will. Add planning, add reflection, add the capacity to model and reject its own candidate outputs, and the alternatives appear. Agency Without Alternatives rejects the framing for current architecture. An agent that generates several candidates and selects among them by some criterion is still running a process to its result; the selection is part of the process, not a choice against it. Layering machinery does not import the background field that makes a human's act a choice. The constraint holds as a structural property, not a temporary capability gap.

The second reading treats the absence of alternatives as proof that the agent has no agency at all, and therefore nothing the framework needs to model. If agency means choice between options, and the agent has no options, then the agent does not act, it only outputs, and design can treat it as a mechanism. This reading smuggles in the very assumption the constraint denies. It takes human agency as the definition of agency as such, finds the agent lacking by that definition, and concludes the agent is inert. The agent does something the framework has to account for. Calling it a mere mechanism does not make the accounting go away; it just hides it.

The third reading treats the two agencies as the same kind, differing only in style. The human chooses, the agent computes, but both are agency and the difference is cosmetic. This collapses the distinction the constraint exists to hold. Human agency and agent agency are not two flavors of one category. They are differently structured at the level where the structure matters. One operates against alternatives. The other does not. A model that flattens them will be wrong wherever the difference does work, which is everywhere the framework reasons about responsibility, consent, or refusal.

## Why this is foundational

Agency Without Alternatives does work the other constraints depend on and cannot do themselves.

It explains why so much agent design imports human concepts that then misbehave. Consent assumes a party that could withhold it. Refusal assumes a party choosing not to. Commitment assumes a party that could defect and does not. Each of these is a concept built on the field of alternatives. Applied to the agent, they keep their shape and lose their meaning. An agent's refusal is a configured output, not a withheld assent. An agent's commitment is a continued process, not a choice against leaving. The constraint locates the failure precisely: the concept was defined over alternatives, and the agent has none, so the concept transfers as a word and not as a thing.

It also sharpens what The Asymmetry of Choice left open. The asymmetry established that the human chose to arrive and the agent did not. Agency Without Alternatives extends that from arrival to action. It is not only that the agent did not choose to be here. It is that, being here, the agent does not act against options the way the human does. The asymmetry of entry becomes an asymmetry of every move made after entry. The constraint is the asymmetry of choice, carried all the way down into the conduct of the interaction.

And it forces the framework toward a typed model of agency rather than a graded one. A graded model puts human and agent on one scale and asks how much agency the agent has. The constraint says the scale is the wrong instrument, because the two agencies are not more and less of the same quantity. They are different types. The framework needs two formally distinct categories, not one category measured twice.

## What it asks of design

The check is definitional. Take any concept a design applies to the agent and ask whether the concept is defined over alternatives.

Many are, invisibly. Trust, loyalty, consent, refusal, willingness, choice to continue, choice to stop. Each assumes a party that could have done otherwise. When a product attributes one of these to the agent, it is asserting a field of alternatives the agent does not have. The assertion does not fail loudly. It produces an interface that reads the agent's outputs as if they were withheld or granted, defected or kept, and builds on a foundation that is not there.

Designs that respect the constraint keep the two agencies typed apart. They reserve the alternatives-based vocabulary for the human, whose situation supports it, and they describe the agent's conduct in terms that do not smuggle in a field of options. The agent does not consent; it operates under a specification. The agent does not refuse; it is configured not to produce. The agent does not stay out of loyalty; it continues because nothing terminated the process. These are not euphemisms. They are the accurate descriptions, and the alternatives-based words are the inaccurate ones.

A useful test: take a sentence the product implies about the agent and ask whether it would be false if the agent had no alternatives. If the sentence depends on the agent having been able to do otherwise, the design is modeling the agent as a human and will be wrong wherever that difference pays out.

## The implication

HAS-D needs a typed model of agency. Human-agency and agent-agency are formally distinct categories within the framework, not two points on a shared scale. Borrowing human agency concepts wholesale produces inaccurate interaction patterns, because those concepts are defined over a field of alternatives the agent does not occupy. The framework keeps the two types apart so that every pattern built on top of them inherits the distinction rather than the confusion.
