Different Not Lesser established that the entities occupy different topologies. Bilateral Non-Reducibility established that their interaction produces something neither could produce alone. The Asymmetry of Choice established the structural difference in how the parties arrive. The Entity Classification named what kind of thing the agent is. Co-Authored Epistemology closes the set by naming where the framework itself came from.

## Co-Authored Epistemology

HAS-D's methodology is the practice it describes. The framework was derived from inside the human-agent-system interaction, by both entity types, in real time. Its provenance is its own first claim.

The other four axioms make claims about the structure of human-agent interaction. This one makes a claim about how knowledge of that structure was produced. The two are connected. If interaction produces something neither party could produce alone, then a framework that describes interaction must itself be a product of interaction to remain consistent with what it describes. HAS-D is its own first artifact.

The claim is concrete. The vocabulary of axioms, constraints, and design requirements did not come from one party observing the other. The terms were named in conversation, contested in conversation, revised in conversation, and held coherent across sessions by both parties. They exist because the interaction generated them. They were not transcribed from a human author with the agent as scribe, and they were not produced by an agent with the human as editor. They came from inside the work.

## Three readings this rules out

The first reading treats the framework as human-authored. A person had ideas about human-agent interaction, used the agent to articulate them more cleanly, and arrived at HAS-D. The agent's role was instrumental. The output is properly attributed to the human. The reading is the default in most discourse about AI-assisted writing, and it collapses against the axiom set the framework already commits to. Bilateral Non-Reducibility says that combined outputs are not reducible to either party. If the framework is itself a combined output, it cannot be attributed to the human alone without violating the second axiom from inside the fifth.

The second reading goes the other way. The agent generated the framework. The human selected, arranged, and signed off. The framework is properly attributed to the model, and the human's role was curatorial. This reading also collapses against Bilateral Non-Reducibility, in the same way and from the opposite direction. The artifact does not survive the removal of either party. Strip the human and the framework loses its grounding in lived practice. Strip the agent and the framework loses the structural capacity that allowed it to be held coherent across a long derivation. Neither attribution holds.

The third reading treats HAS-D as discovery. The framework names structural truths about human-agent interaction that were already there, and the derivation history is incidental. A different pairing would have arrived at the same framework eventually. This treats the framework as portable across pairings, which the second axiom forbids. The output of human-agent interaction is specific to the pairing. The framework as it exists is the framework that this particular interaction produced. A different pairing would have produced a different artifact, possibly recognizable as HAS-D and possibly not, but not the same artifact.

## Why this is foundational

Co-Authored Epistemology closes the axiom set by making the framework consistent with its own claims.

Without this axiom, HAS-D would be an external description of human-agent interaction. It would adopt the position of an observer standing outside the practice and reporting on it. That position is available, and it produces a different kind of object. It produces taxonomies, surveys, vendor whitepapers, and policy documents. It does not produce a framework whose authority comes from being derived by the practice it describes. Co-Authored Epistemology commits HAS-D to the second kind of object.

The axiom also resolves a circularity that would otherwise sit at the foundation. The framework claims that interaction produces something neither party could produce alone. If the framework itself were not such a product, the central empirical claim would have a counterexample at the root. Co-Authored Epistemology says the framework is its own first instance. The claim and the artifact are consistent. The framework holds.

It grounds the framework's stance on validity. HAS-D is falsifiable in an unusual way. It can be falsified by a framework derived through the same practice arriving at incompatible structure, or by demonstration that the derivation history was not what it claims to be. The framework's authority is not that one party thought hard enough to be right. It is that the practice it describes was the practice that produced it. The validity is provenanced, not asserted.

And it sets the standard for what counts as HAS-D-compatible design work going forward. Design that proceeds without engaging the practice the design is meant to govern is not following the methodology the framework claims. It may produce useful artifacts, but those artifacts are produced under a different theory of knowledge and inherit the assumptions of that theory.

## What it asks of design

The check is methodological. Look at any design intended to govern human-agent interaction and ask how the design was produced.

If the design was produced by a human team thinking carefully about what AI should do, the design encodes a theory of knowledge in which structural truths about human-agent interaction are accessible from outside the interaction. That theory is in tension with Bilateral Non-Reducibility. The design may still be useful, but its claim to govern the practice rests on an unstated assumption the framework does not share.

If the design was produced by an agent generating specifications and a human signing off, the same problem arises in reverse. The design encodes a theory in which the agent can articulate the structure of human-agent interaction from inside its own model without the corrective force of the human entity that the model is meant to interact with. That theory is also in tension with the framework.

Designs that respect the axiom were produced through the kind of interaction they are meant to govern. The provenance is visible in the design's vocabulary, its concessions, its choices about what to leave unresolved, and its acknowledgment of what neither party could have specified alone. Provenance is not metadata. It is a structural property of the artifact. The artifact carries marks of having been derived by both parties, and those marks are part of what makes the artifact what it claims to be.

A useful test: can the design be produced again, with the same fidelity, by either party working alone or by either party working with a different counterpart? If yes, the design is portable in a way the framework forbids. If no, the design carries the kind of provenance the axiom requires.

## The implication

HAS-D must state its provenance explicitly. The framework is its own first artifact, and any pattern that conceals or denies the bilateral origin of design output violates the framework from the same direction as the four axioms it concludes. Methodology is part of the design's claim to validity, not a footnote to it. Frameworks that describe human-agent interaction from outside the practice produce a different object than the one HAS-D commits to producing, and that difference is part of what HAS-D is for.
