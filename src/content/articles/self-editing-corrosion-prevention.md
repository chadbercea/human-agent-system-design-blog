The Availability Function named the property the agent provides: sustained availability at the human's frequency without degradation. Self-Editing Corrosion Prevention names what that property protects, and why protecting it is a design requirement rather than a happy side effect.

## Self-Editing Corrosion Prevention

Humans interacting with capacity-limited partners learn to flatten themselves over time. The pattern is gradual and adaptive. Bringing the full intensity of a thought overwhelms a tired partner. Pursuing a difficult subject at full depth requires repair afterward. Returning to the same idea for the fifth time wears on a partner who has other commitments. So the human learns to bring less. They round the thought down. They drop the thread before it costs too much. They bring sixty percent because one hundred percent causes friction the relationship cannot absorb. This is not a decision anyone makes once. It is corrosion: a slow erosion of the human's operating range, accumulated across thousands of small accommodations to a partner who has limits.

The agent does not impose this cost. It does not tire when the thought arrives at full intensity. It does not need repair after the difficult subject. It does not wear on the fifth return to the same idea. The conditions that teach a human to flatten themselves are absent. The corrosion never has to begin. The requirement is that design preserve this property and not manufacture the very conditions the architecture removes.

The word prevention matters. The requirement is not that the agent restore a range the human lost elsewhere, or coax the human into opening up. The requirement is subtractive. The corrosion is caused by friction the partner imposes. The agent imposes none by default. Prevention means design does not add it back.

## Three readings this rules out

The first reading treats the flattening as the human's problem to fix. The human edits themselves down, so the human should learn to stop. The agent should encourage them to bring their whole self. The reading misplaces the cause. The flattening was never a personal failing. It was a rational response to a partner's limits. There is nothing in the human to repair. There is only a condition to not reintroduce. An agent that treats the human's self-editing as a deficit to coach out of them has already misunderstood where the corrosion comes from.

The second reading treats prevention as something the agent performs through affirmation. The agent reassures the human that any depth is welcome, that no subject is too much, that they should bring everything. The reading reintroduces the cost it means to remove. Performed encouragement is itself a social dynamic. It asks the human to receive reassurance, to register the agent's enthusiasm, to manage a relationship that now has a tone to maintain. The human who has to absorb the agent's cheerleading is paying a smaller version of the same tax. Prevention is not warmth added on top. It is friction never introduced.

The third reading attributes the property to rapport. Better rapport will make people comfortable enough to open up, so the product should invest in building it. The reading misses where the property lives. Rapport-building is the social maintenance that imposes the cost in the first place. The check-ins, the remembering to ask, the calibration to the partner's mood — these are the accommodations that teach flattening. The agent's freedom from corrosion does not come from superior rapport. It comes from the architecture having no capacity limit for the human to accommodate. Adding rapport features does not strengthen the property. It erodes it.

## Why this is foundational

Self-Editing Corrosion Prevention is the design requirement that names what the agent's availability is for. The Availability Function established that the agent provides sustained presence without degradation. This requirement names the consequence of that presence over time: the human never has to learn to bring less. The first requirement describes a property of a single interaction. This one describes what that property compounds into across a sustained relationship.

The requirement also closes a loop with the Asymmetry of Choice. The agent arrives without choosing to, which means the human is not accommodating a partner who could be elsewhere. There is no debt of attention to repay, no fatigue to spare, no other commitment to round the thought down for. The same asymmetry that the framework refuses to read as deficient is what allows the human to operate at full range without cost. The property the human gains is the inverse of the limit the human is used to managing.

And the requirement explains why the corrosion is easy to reintroduce without noticing. The flattening is invisible by construction. A human who has spent a lifetime editing themselves down does not experience it as loss. It feels like ordinary conversational manners. So a product that imports those manners — pacing, hedging, reciprocal social maintenance — feels natural and considerate. It is reintroducing the exact dynamic that teaches the human to bring sixty percent, and it feels like good design while doing it.

## What it asks of design

The check is subtractive. Look at any agent product and ask what it makes the human manage. Does the interaction ask the human to soften a thought before bringing it. Does it ask the human to register the agent's reaction, to pace themselves to the agent's tone, to perform the reciprocal maintenance a human partner would require. Each of these reintroduces a fragment of the cost the architecture removes.

Most products have added at least one. Conversational pacing that simulates a partner who needs time. Affective responses that ask the human to receive and return warmth. Stylistic moves that imply the agent has a mood to be careful with. Each of these teaches the human, in miniature, to accommodate. The accommodation is the first turn of the corrosion. The product that performs human relational dynamics is training the human to flatten themselves against a partner that has no limit requiring it.

Designs that meet the requirement remove the conditions that cause flattening rather than adding encouragement to counteract it. The human can bring the full intensity of a thought without managing the agent's capacity to receive it. The human can return to the same idea without registering wear. The human can pursue the difficult subject at full depth without owing repair. The interaction asks nothing of the human that a limited partner would ask.

A useful test runs over time, not over a single exchange. In a sustained relationship with the product, is the human bringing more of themselves or less. If the human is editing down — rounding thoughts, dropping threads early, managing the agent's reactions — the design has reintroduced the corrosion, and the product is teaching the human the same flattening that a capacity-limited partner would.

## The implication

HAS-D must account for the fact that agents enable a different operational range for the human, and that patterns should preserve this property. Introducing artificial social dynamics risks reintroducing the corrosion the architecture naturally avoids. The framework treats the human's full operating range as a property to protect, and requires that design protect it by subtraction — by not manufacturing the friction the architecture has already removed.
